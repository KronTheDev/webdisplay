In the early 2010s, as personal recording devices trended toward cloud dependence and disposable hardware, a countercurrent emerged among journalists, investigators, and emergency services. It favored something simpler. Something that could survive being forgotten.

- **_The Talky was a rugged, single-purpose manual recorder, built around the idea that information mattered most when nothing else worked._**

Inspired by late-1990s consumer recorders yet engineered with industrial tolerances, Talkys stripped away connectivity in favor of physical controls, long-term storage stability, and extreme battery longevity.

- Handheld, cassette-less audio recorders inspired by late-90s consumer tech *(TalkBoy lineage)*.
- Uses the audio-logged [['HarDis' (Advanced Floppy Disks)]] for playback storage; low fidelity containment, but extremely resilient to dust and moisture.
- A maximum of a minute per recording.
- Primary use is *manual testimony recording*: survivor logs, last wills, field reports, confessions — often tied to quests or environmental storytelling.
- Playback is linear only; no fast search or indexing, forcing users to _listen through_ entire recordings.
- Microphones are directional but crude — background noise (wind, ash, coughing) is often present, adding ambiguity.
- Battery-powered via hand-crank or scavenged low-voltage cells; unreliable power incentivizes selective recording.
- Widely trusted due to their *offline, non-networked nature*, unlike [[Public Intranet Terminals (GlobaVision)]] which are often surveilled or inaccessible.
- Often personalized: scratched casings, taped labels, voice imprints — making each Talky feel irreplaceable.
