# GAME IDEA

A grounded post-apocalyptic narrative survival game set after the eruption of Yellowstone, where society is divided vertically between those who fled to the mountains and those forced to survive “Above the Line” within a toxic atmospheric band.

The game explores infrastructure collapse, moral agency, and the will to leave comfort behind in pursuit of uncertain freedom.

Content warning: environmental disaster themes, atmospheric toxicity, survival hardship, moral conflict.

---

# GAME OBJECTIVE

## MAIN OBJECTIVE

To create a grounded, atmospheric survival experience that demonstrates how discipline, structure, and deliberate design can produce a cohesive and emotionally resonant indie game.

---

## GAMEPLAY GOALS

- To make the player feel tense but capable.

- To create slow-burn immersion rather than adrenaline spectacle.

- To encourage thoughtful movement and decision-making.

- To make survival feel systemic, not scripted.

- To evoke the feeling of choosing movement over stagnation.


---

## PRESENTATION GOALS

- To create a restrained, melancholic atmosphere.

- To present decay as quiet erosion.

- To maintain realism in tone, architecture, and systems.

- To ensure UI and audio feel utilitarian and grounded.

- To avoid cinematic exaggeration in favor of environmental storytelling.

# SUMMARY

In late 2039, the Yellowstone supereruption reshaped the atmosphere and fractured society. In Vancouver, verticality became destiny. Toxic sulfuric clouds settled into the lower elevations, creating a hazardous band known as “The Line.”

Some fled to the mountains. Others remained.

The player assumes the role of a survivor living in a partially stable high-rise settlement. While others grow comfortable within limited safety, the protagonist chooses to leave — to travel toward the mountains in search of cleaner air, answers, and autonomy.

The journey unfolds across interconnected urban structures, abandoned terminals, fractured settlements, and environmental hazards shaped by long-term ashfall and atmospheric collapse.

The story is not about saving the world: it is about choosing direction in its aftermath.

# GAMEPLAY

## MECHANICS

- First-person exploration.

- Environmental traversal (stairs, gangways, vertical structures).

- Limited inventory management.

- Air filtration management system.

- Resource scavenging.

- Intranet Terminal interaction (HarDis-based data access).

- Dialogue-based narrative encounters.

- Environmental hazard avoidance (toxic zones, collapse areas).


Progression occurs through:

- Reaching new elevation zones.

- Securing access routes.

- Unlocking Intranet nodes.

- Interacting with settlements and factions.


---

## FEATURES

- Vertical survival environment divided by atmospheric layers.

- HarDis-based physical data storage system.

- Public Intranet Terminals with localized network routing.

- Modular interconnected urban architecture.

- Diegetic UI and grounded HUD.

- Dynamic environmental soundscapes.

- Phase-based development structure ensuring systemic cohesion.


---

# PRESENTATION

## TEXT

Tone: restrained, stoic, introspective.  
Dialogue is minimal and deliberate.  
Terminal logs and recordings carry emotional weight without melodrama.  
Formatting is clean, functional, and minimalistic.

Inspiration:

- Todd Howard (Fallout Franchise Writer / Director).

- Technical documentation tone blended with human memory.


---

## VISUAL

Style:

- Semi-realistic architecture.

- Post-soviet / early 2000s urban expansion influence.

- Concrete-heavy, structural emphasis.

- Muted palette: greys, pale yellows, oxidized greens.


Atmosphere:

- Diffused yellow haze.

- Subtle particulate fog.

- Visible structural decay.


UI:

- Semi-Utilitarian.

- Terminal-inspired layouts.

- Minimal visual clutter.


---

## AUDIO

Sound Design:

- Wind-driven ambience.

- Structural creaks.

- Distant metallic resonance.

- Subtle low-frequency atmospheric pressure tones.


Music:

- Sparse.

- Ambient.

- Minimalistic electronic textures.

- Rarely dominant; environmental audio carries most immersion.


***Audio supports tension through restraint, not volume.***
