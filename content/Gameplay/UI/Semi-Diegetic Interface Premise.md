
In this preset, the interface is not meant to exist as a traditional overlay layered on top of the player's vision. Instead, the experience relies on a semi-diegetic system: information emerges from the player's physical state, equipment, and environment rather than being presented through persistent HUD elements.

## ***Two key constraints shape this approach:***

- *The First is the nature of the perspective itself. The game is strictly first-person, with no third-person alternative. At the same time, the technological stagnation that followed the Y2K collapse prevented the development of advanced AR systems capable of projecting information directly into the user's vision. Outside of basic on-glass projections and simple device screens, no system exists that could realistically overlay digital information onto the player's eyes.*

- *The Second is immersion. The intention is to create a physical experience where the player perceives danger, damage, and environmental threats the same way the character would: through sensory cues, bodily reactions, and environmental distortion rather than abstract indicators.*

As a result, most interface elements are treated as visual or physical impairments rather than traditional HUD widgets.

Instead of health bars or readouts, the player’s condition manifests through perceptible changes in their vision and behavior.

### ***Examples include:***

- **Damage Feedback**  
  A brief red vignette appears at the edges of the screen when the player takes damage. The effect pulses momentarily and fades, communicating injury without persistent UI elements.

- **Low Health Degradation**  
  As health decreases, color saturation gradually fades. Peripheral blur increases slightly, conveying physical weakness and disorientation.

- **Gas Exposure Distortion**  
  When the player inhales toxic gases below the Line, the screen’s color grading shifts toward a sickly yellow-green tone, accompanied by subtle breathing-driven camera sway.

- **Poisoning Progression**  
  Prolonged exposure causes progressive desaturation, slight visual distortion, and intermittent coughing. If untreated, the effect intensifies until the character collapses.

- **Filter Degradation Cue**  
  As the gas mask filter deteriorates, condensation begins to appear on the visor. The fogging slowly spreads from the edges inward, reducing clarity.

- **Oxygen Deprivation**  
  Heavy breathing sounds and subtle darkening at the screen edges appear as oxygen levels fall.

Because of this preset, gameplay systems such as inventory, quick-access items, and task information are not displayed as floating UI panels.

Instead, they are accessed through in-world devices, such as the [['Rogers' (Verter LTD)]] communicator or various terminals scattered throughout the environment.

This separation ensures that moment-to-moment survival feedback remains **sensory and physical**, while structured information remains **device-bound and contextual**.

One of the clearest demonstrations of this design philosophy appears in the player's exposure to the atmosphere below the Line (*As mentioned in [[BTL Player Behaviour]]).

Time spent in these conditions is governed by two primary factors:

- *Filter integrity.*
- *Total body exposure duration.*

As exposure increases beyond safe limits, the player’s vision gradually loses saturation and clarity. Coughing becomes more frequent, breathing grows unstable, and movement becomes increasingly erratic. If the situation persists long enough, the poisoning ultimately culminates in a fatal collapse.
