
The **BTL (Below the Line)** environment represents the most hazardous gameplay layer within the world. It is defined by the presence of the **CL (Cloud)** — a dense atmospheric accumulation of volcanic gases and particulate matter produced by the eruption events.

Within this zone, the player's survival is governed by two primary variables:

- ***FI — Filter Integrity***  
- ***ET — Exposure Time***

Together, these systems determine how long a player can safely remain within the Cloud before suffering severe physiological consequences.

---

## Baseline Environmental Conditions

The Cloud is composed of the **GS (Gas System)** — a mixture of toxic gases, acidic particulates, and sulfuric aerosols.

***Primary effects include:***

- *respiratory distress.*
- *chemical irritation.*
- *neurological impairment.*
- *visual degradation.*

The intensity of these effects varies depending on **Cloud density**, which increases significantly as altitude decreases.

---

## Entering BTL with Standard Equipment

The baseline survival equipment required to enter BTL is a **gas mask with a filtration cartridge**.

***When equipped:***

- *The mask protects the respiratory system from the majority of toxic gases.*
- *However, the rest of the body remains partially exposed to atmospheric contaminants.*


#### ***As the player spends time BTL, the following systems begin to interact:***
### Filter Integrity (FI)

The filtration cartridge gradually deteriorates while processing toxic gases.

***Symptoms of filter degradation include:***

- *condensation building on the mask visor.*
- *increasingly muffled breathing sounds.*
- *subtle distortion in peripheral vision.*
- *increased coughing frequency.*

If **FI reaches critical levels**, the mask can no longer prevent toxic inhalation.

---
### Exposure Time (ET)

***Even with a functioning mask, the body absorbs contaminants through:***

- *skin exposure.*
- *eye irritation (Only if partial mask).*
- *environmental particulates.*

**Prolonged exposure causes progressive poisoning.**

***Symptoms appear in stages:***

1. **Mild Exposure**
   - *slight desaturation of colors.*
   - *occasional cough.*
   - *subtle breathing difficulties.*

1. **Moderate Exposure**
   - *stronger visual desaturation.*
   - *increased coughing.*
   - *reduced stamina recovery.*

3. **Severe Exposure**
   - *unstable movement.*
   - *aggressive coughing fits.*
   - *strong visual distortion.*
   - *loss of color and clarity.*

4. **Critical Exposure**
   - *violent coughing.*
   - *breathing failure.*
   - *collapse and ultimately death.*

---
## Entering BTL Without a Mask

Entering the Cloud without respiratory protection is extremely dangerous, but its lethality depends strongly on **Cloud density** and altitude relative to **AL (At the Line)**.

In the **uppermost BTL layers**, where the Cloud is thinner and less chemically concentrated, unprotected exposure does not result in immediate death. However, the gases still cause rapid irritation and physiological stress.

Within seconds of entering these lighter layers without a mask, the player begins to experience:

- irritation of the eyes and throat  
- involuntary coughing  
- slight visual desaturation  
- shallow breathing and discomfort  

At this level, the player may survive **roughly 30–60 seconds** before the symptoms escalate. During this time, movement becomes increasingly unstable as coughing intensifies and breathing grows more strained.

If the player retreats back **ATL** quickly enough, recovery is possible after a short period of coughing and blurred vision.

However, remaining within the Cloud longer quickly pushes exposure into dangerous territory.

---
## Cloud Density Levels

The Cloud does not behave uniformly. Instead, it forms vertical density layers depending on altitude.

***These layers significantly influence survivability and equipment requirements.***



### Light Density (Upper BTL)

***Average Height:***
Located near **AL (At the Line)**, thus **>8 Story**.

***Characteristics:***

- *slower FI consumption.*
- *longer safe exposure times.*

***Typical survival time with a mask:***

**20–30 minutes**

***Equipment required:***

- *gas mask.*
- *standard filtration cartridge.*



### Moderate Density (Lower BTL)

***Average Height (h):***

**5 < h < 8 Stories**.

***Characteristics:***

- *increased gas concentration.*
- *visible particulate matter.*
- *noticeable breathing strain.*
- *faster filter degradation.*

***Typical survival time:***

**10–15 minutes**

***Recommended equipment:***

- *upgraded filtration cartridge.*
- *protective clothing.*
- *eye protection.*
.
---

### Heavy Density / Sulphur Layer (SL)

***Average Height (h):***

**2.5 < h < 5 Stories**.

***Characteristics:***

- *thick yellow atmospheric haze.*
- *strong chemical irritation.*
- *heavy breathing difficulty.*
- *extremely rapid filter degradation.*

***Typical survival time:***

**5–8 minutes**

***Required equipment:***

- *high-capacity filtration mask.*
- *partial body protection.*
- *sealed gloves and eye protection.*

---

### Extreme Density / Dead Zone (DZ)

These areas represent the densest Cloud accumulation zones.

***Average Height (h):***

**0 < h < 3 Stories**.

***Characteristics:***

- *extremely poor visibility.*
- *constant coughing.*
- *rapid poisoning.*
- *severe visual impairment.*

***Typical survival time:***

**2–4 minutes**

***Required equipment:***

- *advanced filtration system.*
- *reinforced respirator.*
- *sealed breathing tank.*
- ***full protective suit.***

---

## Full Protective Suit Operations

At the deepest Cloud levels, survival requires a **sealed environmental suit**.

***These suits provide:***

- *full-body chemical protection.*
- *sealed breathing system.*
- *internal filtration systems.*
- *extended exposure tolerance.*

***However, they introduce gameplay limitations:***

- *reduced mobility.*
- *slower stamina regeneration.*
- *heavier movement.*
- *limited operational time due to set oxygen reserves.*

***Even with a full suit, the Cloud remains dangerous.***
