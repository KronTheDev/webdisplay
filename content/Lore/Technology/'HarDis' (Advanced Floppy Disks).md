In the mid-2020s, as conventional data storage began to buckle under growing infrastructural strain, a quieter solution emerged from a consensus of GlobaVision child-companies, to be implemented within public, corporate and governmental intranets.

-  **_The Hardened Data Disk (HarDis) was designed as a physically typed storage medium, prioritizing stability and control over flexibility, effectively redefining information storage and transmition._**

Unlike general-purpose digital storage, HarDis units were manufactured with immutable, pre-defined formats, each tailored to a specific class of data. This architectural rigidity drastically reduced corruption, misuse, and cascading system failures, making them ideal for controlled networks and critical information exchange.

On the technical side, they were advanced, ruggedized data storage media manufactured with *fixed, non-modifiable pre-formatted types*, each optimized for a specific class of data; format type is physically encoded at fabrication and determines file structure, read/write logic and terminal compatibility.

***Types:***
- *Audio (HarDis-A):*  
    Stores voice and low-bandwidth sound data only; supports sequential playback and timestamp anchoring; highly resilient to partial corruption, often retaining intelligible fragments even when damaged; commonly used for [[Talky(s) (Manual Recorders)]] recordings, emergency logs, and oral archives.
- *Media (HarDis-M):*  
    Stores static visual media such as images, schematics, and scanned documents; limited, degraded video support on high-grade Terminals; corruption typically results in missing layers, visual tearing, or incorrect scaling; used by engineers, archivists, and survey teams.
- *File (HarDis-F):*  
    General-purpose digital storage for written logs, code fragments, and configuration data; access and modification governed by pre-attributed authorization by the user; often encrypted or fragmented; highly valued by factions controlling infrastructure and information flow.
- *Routing (HarDis-R):*  
    Specialized disk containing intranet routing tables, access cache, and authentication keys; required to connect terminals beyond local systems; relatively rare and very difficult to duplicate due to a one-sided type of encoding.

Most HarDis' *(read "Har-dies" in plural)*, except for *R-Types*, can be re-configured through a special developer access terminal, called the *Re-Bind Terminal* (link soon).

By the early 2030s, HarDis technology had become a staple across secured terminals, municipal systems, and industrial networks, particularly in regions where redundancy and survivability outweighed convenience.

In the post-Ashfall world, HarDis disks remain one of the few reliable means of storing, transporting, and routing information.
